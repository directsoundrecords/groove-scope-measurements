What the eye missed

Michelangelo Canonico measured the Rega for Direct Sound Records. Thanks to Mauro Neri, director of AudioReview, for making the laboratory session possible.

01 · Azimuth with Groove Scope

The leakage gap narrowed

10.27 dB→to1.16 dB

Viewed head-on, the Carbon appeared aligned. Groove Scope measured how much of a 1 kHz tone crossed into the opposite channel in each direction. As found, far more of the right channel leaked into the left than the other way around.

With azimuth adjusted, right-to-left leakage dropped by 7.7 dB while left-to-right leakage rose by 1.3 dB. Average separation improved from 28.6 to 31.8 dB. The left channel’s output advantage edged up to +1.04 dB.

02 · Trackability with Groove Scope Studio

What half a gram unlocked

60 µm→to80 µm

In a separate tracking trial, the Carbon reached 60 µm at 2.0 g. At 2.5 g it reached 80 µm, one-third more groove modulation on this test.

Both settings fall within the Carbon’s specified 2–3 g range. The 80 µm result describes this cartridge and test session. Rega Carbon specifications ↗

Review summary

Two adjustments, two measurable gains

The Carbon came out of the session with 31.8 dB average separation at 1 kHz and an 80 µm result in the tracking trial. A cartridge can look straight and still leak much more in one direction. On this Rega, measuring both directions gave the azimuth adjustment a clear target, and the tracking ladder made the gain from half a gram visible.
